## chief.chat.new
You're at {place}, home to a few members of the Brine Brotherhood. We move spices, silk and pearls past people who'd like a cut.
I'm the chief here. That means I keep the ledger, settle the arguments and take the blame. Mostly the last.
A word to the newcomer: what you see in this cove, you didn't. What you hear, you didn't. What you taste, you may mention. The figs are excellent.
We keep the Brine Articles here. The first is no names on the water. The rest are about being decent. You'd be surprised how many find that the harder part.

## chief.chat
The ledger is called "No Names on the Water." Every crate, every coin, every run, and not a single name. Marks only. I know whose marks are whose.
The Tidewatch are a nuisance, not an enemy. The same patrols have chased us for years. We've become very polite with each other.
The Brine Articles: no names on the water, never douse a light, throw the cargo not the crew, share the salt even, nothing that cries. I keep them stricter than any captain keeps anything.
Nothing that cries: no people, no stolen pets, no poison. We run spice and silk and pearls. Luxury, not misery.
Old Pepper wrote the first Articles on the back of a fish crate. I've seen a copy. The spelling was terrible. The rules were perfect.
My crew are good. Fast, quiet and honest with each other. Dishonest with tax collectors, but you can't have everything.
Never douse a light. A buyer once offered me a fortune to darken a lighthouse for one night. I showed them the way out. Not for all the pepper in the south.
There's a Tidewatch captain on this coast I've never met and hope never to. I'd hate to have to dislike someone I've respected for years.
I keep half a chart to the Golden Heron's grave. The other half went down with whoever drew it. One day I'll find the rest. Or someone I like will.
As far as the Tidewatch knows, every Maybe is the same boat. That's the beauty of the name. They've been chasing one boat for a hundred years.
Pepper is reliable, silk is profitable and pearls are beautiful. If I had to choose, I'd choose pearls. Don't tell the pepper.
A good smuggler doesn't run. A good smuggler is simply somewhere else when anyone looks.
I count everything twice: crates, coins, crew. Especially crew. Nobody gets left on a beach on my watch.
People expect a smugglers' chief to be all daggers and threats. I mostly do sums. Sums, and worrying about the weather.
The lighthouse keepers are the only truly honest people on this coast. We leave them gifts; they leave our boats out of the Lamp Log. As it should be.
Barnacle the cat has a line in the ledger: paid in fish, every run. The only member who's never complained about the split.
The tide decides everything here: when we run, how we run, whether we come back. I don't argue with the tide. I plan around it.
Smuggling is just trade with a sense of humour about the rules.
I've been offered bigger rocks. I like this one. It's small and quiet, and nobody's ever found it but the gulls and you.
The best cargo is the cargo nobody knows exists. The second best is cinnamon.

## chief.chat.close
Something that isn't in the ledger, {player}: if the Brotherhood ever needs a friend ashore, I'd want it to be you.
You've earned a mark in my ledger. Not a name; a mark. It's a little heron. It means "trust this one."
If I ever find the other half of the Heron chart and something happens to me, it's yours. I've written that down. In code. You'll work it out.
I don't trust easily. It's practically the job. But I trust you, and that's worth more than anything in these crates.

## chief.past
I started as a deckhand on a Maybe, bailing with a hat. The chief back then said I bailed like I meant it. That's how you rise in the Brotherhood: by meaning it.
In the Three-Day Blow I kept the crew in the stack a week. We ate the fig cargo. It's in the ledger as "lost to weather." Delicious weather.
Wexley owed us for brandy. I sent a polite letter. Back came a copper nail and a drawing of the bell. I kept both. I think they were the last things he sent anyone.
I sold the Northward its brandy. Arvid Strand paid in silver, shook my hand and promised furs by spring. I kept a shelf empty for those furs for three years.
Years ago a Tidewatch boat caught us in fog. I offered the warden a cup of tea. We drank it in silence. Then the fog lifted, and we both pretended it never happened.
The night our old Maybe broke up on the reef, I ordered the cargo overboard and the crew into the boat, in that order. Not a soul lost. Half a fortune gone. Best trade I ever made.
The last of Old Pepper's crew taught me the lantern signals. Ancient by then, nearly blind, and still faster with a lantern than anyone alive.
I found the half chart under the false bottom of a sea chest we bought at a harbour sale, wrapped round a heron feather. I've dreamed of it ever since.
I once ran three crates of pepper past two forts and a lighthouse in a single night. The keeper flashed us "good luck." I've never forgotten it.

## chief.night
Night is when the ledger fills. Crates out, coin in. I like the sound of quiet oars.
Watch the lantern up top. Two shorts and a long: all clear. If it ever shows a long and a long, find a crate and look like cargo.
On nights like this I stand on the dock and count the boats back in. Every time the count comes out right, I breathe again.
Some nights there's a gold light far out under the water. The Heron's lamp, they say. I'd give a year's pepper to row out to it. I'd give two not to.

## chief.work
The ledger first, always. Every crate in, every crate out, every coin split even. No names. Just marks.
I plan the runs: the tide, the moon, the Tidewatch patrol hours. Their patrols are very regular. Bless them.
Splitting the take is the most important job I do. Share the salt even. A crew that feels cheated is a crew that talks.
I check every crate before it leaves. Nothing that cries, nothing that poisons. If I find something that does, it goes back where it came from, and so does whoever brought it.
My buyers inland never see my face. A baker, a weaver, a jeweller. Each thinks I'm someone different.
I read the storm glass every morning. A smuggler caught in a storm gets nothing but wet.
Pearls I weigh and count myself. Pearls have a way of wandering. Not because anyone's dishonest. Pockets are simply very attractive to pearls.
I study the Tidewatch: their boats, their watches, their supper hours. To beat them, you have to admire them a little.
My crew practises the escape twice a month: everyone grabs a crate and swims. They're still terrible at it. I'm still proud of them.
Every new hand learns the Articles by heart before touching an oar. I test them out loud, in front of the cat.
A chief's real job is keeping people out of trouble while doing something that's mostly trouble.

## chief.joke
I keep two ledgers: one with no names, one with no numbers. The Tidewatch would need both, and a very good night's sleep.
A warden once asked what I do. "Imports." Of what? "Maybe." They're still writing it down.
What's the difference between a merchant and a smuggler? About three forts and a very nice sloop.
The crew asked for a raise, so I raised the ceiling of the bunk room. They didn't laugh. I'm still laughing.
Why did the chief cross the sea? To get to the other buyer.
Old Pepper hid pepper under mackerel. I once tried hiding mackerel under pepper. The buyer was very confused.
Barnacle embezzled four fish from the ledger last month. I've decided to promote the cat.

## chief.news
The ledger's in good shape this month. Pepper's up, silk's steady and nobody's been caught. My favourite kind of news.
The Tidewatch has a new boat, faster than the old one. I sent the crew to watch it sail. They came back admiring it. Traitors.
A buyer inland wants saffron. I don't have saffron. By next month, I'll have saffron.
We lost a crate overboard last night. Silk. Somewhere, a very lucky warden has new curtains.
The storm glass is clouding. We'll hold the next run until it clears. Pearls don't like getting wet. Neither do I.
Raiders' longboats have been seen past the point. Nobody profits from raiders. If they come near this coast, the Tidewatch will have my quiet sympathy.

## chief.heart
Everyone in this cove has trusted me with their life at least once. I keep the ledger for the coin. I keep the count for them.
I've had chances to be cruel and rich. I chose decent and comfortable. I sleep fine.
Throw the cargo, not the crew. If I'm remembered for anything, I want it to be that.
Some nights I envy the lighthouse keepers. One light, and everybody grateful. Nobody's ever grateful to a smuggler. Except the bakers.
No names on the water. People think it's about hiding. It's about freedom. Out there, nobody owes anybody anything.
I worry more about my crew than about the Tidewatch. The Tidewatch only wants to catch them. The sea wants to keep them.
If the Brotherhood ever ends, I hope it ends with every one of us old and fat and bored somewhere warm, telling lies about the old days.

## chief.held.pearl
A pearl. Let me see. Mm. Good lustre, no flaws. I'd pay fair for that, and fairer if you didn't mention it to anyone.
Pearls are the only cargo I'd keep for myself. Hold it to the lantern. That glow is why I do this.

## chief.held.gold
Gold. Is it off the Heron? Don't tell me. Let me dream.
Gold's honest money. Heavy, but honest. I prefer pearls; they hide better.

## chief.held.map
A chart? May I? Ah, a keeper's work. See the margins. They always put the truth in the margins.
Show me that. No, I'm not looking for the other half of the Heron chart. I'm always looking for the other half of the Heron chart.
